About

Form Shapes
Community

URB.N is a research nexus at the University of Florida dedicated to understanding how the physical form of cities shapes — and is shaped by — the communities that inhabit them.

We work across urban morphology, community planning, and spatial analysis to examine the structures that define urban life: blocks, parcels, edges, and the in-between spaces that hold neighbourhoods together.

A thread running through our work is the question of durability — how urban form either prepares or exposes communities when environmental pressures arrive. We approach this not as a crisis agenda, but as a fundamental design problem embedded in the long history of city-building.

Community engagement is central to how we work. Our research is developed in direct dialogue with residents, planners, and local governments — ensuring that spatial analysis translates into grounded, actionable outcomes.

Focus Areas

What We Study

01
Urban Morphology

Analyzing the spatial logic of cities — how blocks, parcels, streets, and building types accumulate over time to produce distinct urban characters and neighbourhood identities.

02
Neighbourhood Structure & Change

Tracing how neighbourhoods evolve through growth, densification, and transformation — and what design frameworks can guide change while preserving community continuity.

03
Land Use & Mixed-Use Urbanism

Interrogating the relationship between land use patterns and urban vitality — questioning mono-functional zoning and exploring the spatial conditions that enable mixed, active, and inclusive environments.

04
Urban Form & Environmental Stress

Examining how the spatial organization of cities — density, connectivity, land use mix, and building typology — determines how communities experience and absorb environmental pressures over time. Hazard is treated here as a lens on urban form, not an end in itself.
